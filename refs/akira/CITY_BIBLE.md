# CITY BIBLE: painted megacity rules (AKIRA-era, 1988)

These rules come from 1,496 frames sampled every 5 s (category lists are in `out/study/city_frames.json`), plus close study of about 30 full-size frames. Frame ids in brackets are evidence, not shots to copy. Hex values were sampled from the frames. Expect them to be about 5% off because of film transfer and JPEG compression.

## A. Urban form

1. **There are three building strata, and every shot mixes at least two.**
   - **Old city (foreground):** 3–8 storey concrete boxes, roughly 1:1 to 1:2 width to height. They have balconies, louvred shutters, tiled awnings, external stairs, AC units, pipes and shop fronts [00022, 00065, 00327, 00326, 00920].
   - **1980s office slabs (midground):** 15–40 storeys, about 1:3. Each has a regular punched-window grid and a two-storey base with columns [00531, 00960].
   - **Neo-Tokyo megatowers (background):** 80–150+ storeys, 1:5 to 1:9, always seen above or behind the other two strata [00048, 00083, 00065].
2. **Megatower vocabulary.** Build each tower from 2–4 stacked prisms. Put setbacks at about ⅓ and ⅔ of the height. Corners are chamfered or rounded. Typical parts:
   - stacked cylinders or drums with horizontal ribbon windows [00048 right, 00083];
   - stepped ziggurat crowns, and flared or overhanging "cap" storeys [00065 teal drum];
   - glowing belt-courses (horizontal bands every 8–15 floors);
   - paired slabs joined by a bridge or gate;
   - thin spires and antenna masts, 5–15% of tower height.
   No two adjacent towers share a silhouette.
3. **The skyline reads as a massif.** Heights rise toward one central core and fall off at the edges. Frame the core between two dark near walls so it reads as a mountain at the end of the street [00065, 00081, 00083]. Seen from across water, the skyline is a 1:6 low wide band with 3–6 needle peaks [00196, 01222].
4. **Density is total.** In aerials, buildings cover more than 90% of the ground. Streets are narrow slots, and only the elevated expressway cuts a clean line through [00005, 00006]. Leave no parks or empty lots, except designed plaza decks.
5. **Streets.**
   - Old-city streets are 2–4 lanes with sidewalks, at about 1.2–1.6 times building height (an enclosed canyon) [00960, 00022].
   - Megatower streets are deep slots, 10:1 height to width, seen from above as a glowing river of car lights [00050, 00387].
   - Elevated pedestrian decks sit between towers. They have patterned paving in red, salmon and ochre geometry, raised planters with trees, a fountain, and lamp columns with red or green caps [00387, 00402, 00527].
6. **Highways.**
   - Elevated expressways stand on single-column T-piers or portal frames painted teal-green (#1f5a55) [00326, 00336].
   - Concrete parapets carry access ladders and a strip of shrubs [00081, 00083].
   - Lamp posts are evenly spaced. Seen in perspective they become a dotted line of halos converging on the vanishing point.
   - Paint lane dashes, zebra and chevron hatching, and yellow-black hazard guards.
   - Ramps fork and curve in aerials [00005]. Toll plazas and parking decks sit on top of roofs [00137].
7. **Rooftop clutter (every roof, 3–8 items):** water tanks (paired cylinders), AC condensers, stair penthouses, lattice masts, billboard frames (a full printed billboard on 1 roof in 5) [00137], cooling ducts, and red aviation lights on every tower top [00787, 00048].
8. **Signage and lettering.**
   - Vertical Japanese sign boards stack on poles and walls [00065, 00960].
   - Shop signage stays in the bottom 15–25% of a facade. At night it is neon tube lettering in magenta, cyan or yellow, sometimes on giant 3D figure signs [00049, 00099].
   - Big Latin-script brand words go on painted billboards [00137, 00327].
   - Graffiti tags are painted with drips on construction hoardings [00327].
   - All lettering is hand-painted: slightly uneven baselines, with flat fill plus one shadow colour.
9. **Utilities and street furniture.** Overhead wires sag between poles in catenaries. Use curved-arm street lamps, traffic signals, round regulatory signs, fire hydrants, chain-link fences, corrugated hoardings, crash barriers, traffic cones, and burned-out cars [00022, 00320, 00326].

## B. How the backgrounds are painted

10. **Opaque poster-paint planes.** Each building face is a flat plane of one colour. Near facades use 3–4 tones (lit face, shadow face, a darker soffit or top band, plus grime). Midground uses 2 tones. Far towers use 1 tone plus an airbrush gradient. Avoid smooth physical shading.
11. **No ink lines on architecture.** Backgrounds define edges only by value and hue contrast. Black outlines belong to cels (characters, vehicles). Line-like marks on buildings are painted 1–2 px rim highlights on corners and ledges, in a lighter or neon tint of the scene's light colour [00048 tower edges in pink and teal].
12. **Airbrush appears in only five places:**
    - the sky gradient;
    - glow at the base of far towers, which are lit from below by city haze and fade upward [00196, 00083];
    - light pools on the ground;
    - halos round light sources;
    - searchlight beams and smoke.
    Near walls are never airbrushed.
13. **Windows depend on distance.**
    - **Near (window over 15 px):** portrait rectangles about 1:1.3 with a lighter frame or sill. A lit window is pale chartreuse or cream (#b4c98d, #d8dca0) with dark interior silhouettes of blinds, furniture or a figure in its lower third, plus a soft glow about 1.5× its size [00531]. Unlit windows are 10–15% darker than the wall (#093534 window on a #073030 wall). In office slabs at night, 60–80% are lit, with whole dark bays and floors.
    - **Mid (3–15 px):** horizontal dashes in rows. Lengths vary from 1 to 6 window-widths, with gaps and the odd dark row. Colours are cyan, white or yellow within the tower's hue family [00048, 00083].
    - **Far (under 3 px):** stippled or sponged glitter dots in horizontal striations, so a tower reads as a glowing column of horizontal lines [00573, 00787: #fdeeb2 / #e8c38f on #710f15].
    - **Very far:** no windows at all, only glow shapes [00196].
14. **Grime.** Old-city walls get vertical rust and water streaks from every sill and joint, cracks, peeling posters, dirty bases and dry-brush texture [00022, 00327]. Megatowers stay clean and glossy. Ruins get cracked slabs, exposed rebar and broken glass shown as jagged dark shapes. Roads get crack lines, stains and litter.
15. **Detail falls off by layer.** Foreground has the most detail: rubbish, cans, bricks. Midground keeps masses and window rhythm. Background keeps only silhouette and glow. The opening aerial [00005] is the exception: it is detailed all the way back, in muted watercolour-like tones.
16. **Edges are slightly soft and hand-ruled.** Long verticals are straight but never laser-crisp, and colour edges have a 1 px soft or chalky transition. Small things wobble: trees are dabbed blobs, smoke is cel-shaded in 2–3 flat tones [00146, 00097].

## C. Light and colour

17. **The night sky is not blue.** It is warm near-black (#120c07, #100b06) [00196, 00787], or crimson glow fading to black (#7e3136 → #120a08) [00048], or desaturated blue-violet (#606d91) going to plum (#322d43) near the skyline [00083]. Keep the darkest values above pure black. Shadows are coloured (#0b1414, #1f1927).
18. **Each tower gets its own hue family.** Pick from teal (#073030 / #135659 / #21827c), plum (#2e1c2a / #503242), crimson (#451120 / #621c2d), indigo (#243b4c / #436675) and amber (#710f15 base with #e8c38f lights). Alternate warm and cool between neighbours so they separate without outlines [00048, 00083].
19. **Night streets use pools and halos, not ambient light.** Lamps are a solid white core with an airbrush halo 3–5× its radius [00022, 00081]. Light pools on asphalt are soft ellipses, flattened by perspective to about 1:4 [00052, 00078]. Neon throws coloured spill onto adjacent walls only. The rest stays dark.
20. **Searchlights and lasers.** Use 4–6 straight beams per skyline, slightly tapered, near-white with soft edges, plus 1–3 thin saturated cyan or magenta lines [00048]. Beams cross and are never volumetric-noisy.
21. **Haze is layered by depth.** Use 3–5 depth planes.
    - At night, each further plane is lighter and more saturated at its base. Glow rises from the street, so the bottom of the far skyline is brightest [00083 haze #826f82].
    - By day, each further plane is lighter, bluer or lavender, and lower in contrast, and the farthest towers nearly dissolve (#c4cfef far vs #beaec1 near) [01444].
22. **Water and wet ground.** The skyline reflects in the bay as vertical streaks of each light's colour, broken by horizontal ripple gaps [00196]. Puddles take mirrored neon blobs [00038]. Floodwater is flat grey-violet with soft reflections [01420].
23. **Day palettes are muted and cool.**
    - Grey-green streets (#515b56, #6f756e, #a7a7a0) [00960]; overcast grey sky (#97a1aa) [00920]; rust and brick accents (#b8674a, #964233).
    - The late-film sky is distinctly green-grey (#547262 → #7e8b7d) [01033], and sunset is crimson-violet [01189].
    - Cast shadows are hard-edged flat shapes about 30–40% darker, with no penumbra [00326, 00916].

## D. Perspective and layout

24. **Use three dominant setups.**
    - **One-point street or highway canyon.** The vanishing point sits on the centre axis with the horizon at 60–72% of frame height. Camera height is about 1–1.5 m, often lower [00960, 00326, 01443].
    - **Worm's-eye.** The camera sits at road level with the horizon at 85–95% of frame height, so towers fill the top 80% and their verticals converge slightly [00083, 00065].
    - **Bird's-eye.** Use a steep three-point view down a canyon, with verticals converging hard to a point below frame [00050, 00387, 00788].
25. **Skylines are orthographic flats.** Distant skylines and window-walls are frontal elevations with zero convergence, stacked as parallax layers. This is telephoto compression [00531, 00196, 00787]. The skyline horizon sits at 50–60% of frame height.
26. **Lens feel.** Street shots look like 28–35 mm, skylines like 85–200 mm telephoto, and aerials like 24 mm looking straight down. Perspective is ruled carefully but by hand. Small inconsistencies are fine. Fisheye distortion never appears.
27. **Composition.** Use strong symmetry for canyons, and a dark near-frame on one or both sides (a building edge, girder or pillar) [00196, 00326]. Sightlines run 1–5 km on the street and 20 km or more in aerials. Each shot holds one hero light source.

## E. What makes code look like CG, and the fix

28. **Uniform window grids with random on/off squares.** Fix this with the rule-13 distance classes: dashes, clusters, dark floors, and interior silhouettes up close.
29. **Physically based shading and smooth falloff.** Quantize every face to 2–4 flat tones, and put gradients only on atmosphere (rule 12).
30. **Toon outlines on buildings.** Do not draw them. Separate shapes with value and hue, and add sparse rim highlights.
31. **Exponential depth fog.** Use discrete haze planes, and a height gradient that glows from the bottom at night.
32. **Global bloom.** Apply bloom only to lamps, neon and searchlights. Windows get a small local glow, and walls never glow.
33. **Pixel-perfect edges and identical repeats.** Add 0.5–1 px edge softness and line jitter. Give each tower its own silhouette and hue, and vary floor heights by ±10%.
34. **Clean surfaces.** Add rust streaks below sills, stained bases, posters and graffiti to all old-city stock.
35. **Saturated blue night sky and pure-black shadows.** Use warm or teal near-black, and keep shadows tinted.
36. **Continuous 3D camera parallax.** The film moves across painted flats. Render 3–6 layered planes, as on a multiplane stand, and slide them at different speeds rather than orbiting a model.
37. **Too clean an image.** Add mild film grain (about 2–3% luminance), a slight gate weave, and very slight colour breathing between shots. Lift blacks to about #0a0806.
